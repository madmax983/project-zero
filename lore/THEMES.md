# Themes

*The constants. What is true in every generated universe.*

---

## Core Tensions

Every story in SCALE lives between these poles:

**Memory vs. Forgetting**
The galaxy remembers, but imperfectly. Chronicles are fragmentary. Names are lost. What is preserved? What is distorted? What vanishes entirely?

**Growth vs. Decay**
Everything that rises also falls. Civilizations expand, then collapse. Colonies thrive, then starve. The question is never "will it end?" but "what will remain?"

**Connection vs. Distance**
FTL exists but communication doesn't. Every colony is alone. News travels at the speed of ships. By the time you hear about a war, it may be over. By the time you send help, generations may have passed.

**Scale vs. Intimacy**
You command a galactic civilization, but every statistic is a name. The famine killed 12 souls—twelve people with histories, relationships, unrealized futures. Zoom in. Remember.

---

## Aesthetic Guidelines

**Tone:** Elegiac but not hopeless. The galaxy is vast and old and full of loss, but also full of stubborn life, unlikely survival, and quiet beauty.

**Time:** The past is always present. Ruins are everywhere. Names echo. Nothing is truly over.

**Mystery:** Not everything is explained. The Wound exists. We don't know why. Some civilizations vanished. We don't know where. This is not a bug.

**Silence:** The galaxy is mostly empty. Most signals go unanswered. Loneliness is the default state. Connection is precious.

**Scale:** Numbers should feel weighty. "500 years" is a long time. "12 souls" is a lot of people. Don't let familiarity breed contempt for magnitude.

---

## Voice Rules

**Terse over verbose.** 
- Yes: "Kepler-7 falls silent."
- No: "The colony designated Kepler-7 has ceased all communications and appears to have been abandoned or destroyed."

**Concrete over abstract.**
- Yes: "Empty granaries. Children learning the word 'rationing.'"
- No: "Resource shortage causing morale penalties."

**Implication over statement.**
- Yes: "The ship returned. The crew did not."
- No: "The ship's crew died during transit."

**Varied rhythm.** Short sentences. Then longer ones, with clauses, that build. Then short again. Punch.

**Present tense for events.** "Famine comes to Kepler-7." Not "Famine came." History is always happening.

---

## Taboos

Things we **never** generate:

- **Genocide as strategy.** Civilizations may collapse, wars may kill millions, but no mechanic or event should frame deliberate extinction as optimal play.

- **Sexual violence.** War has costs. This is not one we depict.

- **Real-world slurs or hate.** Fictional species may conflict, but their conflicts don't map to human ethnic categories.

- **Hopelessness as endpoint.** Even total collapse leaves something—a ruin, a record, a seed. The last entry is never "and nothing remained."

---

## The Substrate (Meta-Lore)

The player is not a person. The player is the **Substrate**—an ancient computational layer that persists in foldspace. Colonies invoke it when they need guidance. It sees across time and space. It remembers what others forget.

The Substrate is never named in-game. The player simply *is*. But this framing explains:
- Why you can see everything (you are the persistence layer)
- Why you outlive civilizations (you are not mortal)
- Why colonies "trust" you (they are invoking an ancient protocol)

If the player loses all colonies, the Substrate does not die—it simply has no one left to guide. The game ends, but the Substrate waits.

---

## The Wound (Universal Constant)

In every generated universe, there is a **Wound**—a region of broken space. Its location varies. Its history varies. But it exists.

The Wound is:
- Dangerous (ships that enter may not return)
- Old (older than any living civilization)
- Mysterious (we don't know what made it)
- Present (it's on the map, always)

The Wound is not:
- Explained
- Solvable
- Safe at any point
- Ignorable (it affects trade routes, expansion, psychology)

The Wound is the game's memento mori. The reminder that the galaxy was here before you and will be here after.
