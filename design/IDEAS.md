## Pop Memory

**Layer:** 1

**Fantasy:** Pops remember what happened to them. A pop who survived a famine distrusts food supplies. A pop who was in the first colony ship has prestige.

**Mechanic:** Pops accumulate "memories" from events they witness. Memories influence behavior and mood. Shared memories create culture.

**Emergence:** Veterans of hard times might be more resilient—or more traumatized. Colony identity forms from shared experience.

**Tension:** Do you shield pops from hardship or let struggle forge character?

## Named Locations

**Layer:** 1

**Fantasy:** Places earn names from what happened there. "Famine Field." "The Old Mine." "Founders' Square."

**Mechanic:** Tiles where significant events occur get named. Names persist. Pops reference names. Player can see history by clicking a named place.

**Emergence:** Your colony develops geography that tells its story. New colonists learn the lore.

**Tension:** None—pure flavor, but makes the world feel lived-in.

## The Void Between

**Layer:** 2 → 3

**Fantasy:** Interstellar travel is dangerous. Ships can be lost. Sometimes they come back... changed.

**Mechanic:** Ships traveling between stars have a small chance of "incidents." Lost ships might reappear years later with strange cargo, changed crews, or warnings.

**Emergence:** Players dread certain routes. Legends form around ghost ships. Successful crossings feel earned.

**Tension:** Fast expansion vs. safe consolidation. Do you send the colony ship through the dangerous route?

## Material-Dependent Construction

**Layer:** 1

**Fantasy:** Building a fortress out of granite feels different than building it out of scrap metal.

**Mechanic:** Buildings don't have fixed costs (e.g. "10 Minerals"). They accept materials with matching tags (e.g. "Structural"). The specific material used imparts stats (HP, insulation, beauty) to the building.

**Emergence:** Slums built from wood are fire hazards. The palace built from marble increases local happiness. Players hunt for rare materials not just for trade, but to build better walls.

**Tension:** Do you build now with poor materials, or wait for the quarry to produce the good stuff?

## The Gravity Well

**Layer:** Cross-layer (1 → 2)

**Fantasy:** The hardest part of space travel is getting off the ground.

**Mechanic:** Moving resources from Layer 1 (Surface) to Layer 2 (System) requires infrastructure (Space Elevators, Mass Drivers, Rockets). Each has throughput limits and costs. It creates a bottleneck.

**Emergence:** You have a breadbasket world overflowing with food, but your starving mining colony can't get it because you lack the launch capacity.

**Tension:** Investing in surface production vs. investing in the logistics to move it.

## Cultural Drift

**Layer:** 3

**Fantasy:** The empire gets too big, and the colonies start to feel... foreign.

**Mechanic:** Colonies drift culturally over time based on distance from the capital and local events. If drift exceeds a threshold, loyalty drops, and they may demand autonomy or secede.

**Emergence:** A distant, prosperous colony develops a pacifist culture while the core is militarist, leading to a political crisis.

**Tension:** Do you enforce conformity (expensive, lowers happiness) or allow local culture (risks secession)?
