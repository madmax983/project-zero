## Pop Memory

**Layer:** 1

**Fantasy:** Pops remember what happened to them. A pop who survived a famine distrusts food supplies. A pop who was in the first colony ship has prestige.

**Mechanic:** Pops accumulate "memories" from events they witness. Memories influence behavior and mood. Shared memories create culture.

**Emergence:** Veterans of hard times might be more resilient—or more traumatized. Colony identity forms from shared experience.

**Tension:** Do you shield pops from hardship or let struggle forge character?

## Named Locations

**Layer:** 1

**Fantasy:** Places earn names from what happened there. "Famine Field." "The Old Mine." "Founders' Square."

**Mechanic:** Tiles where significant events occur get named. Names persist. Pops reference names. Player can see history by clicking a named place.

**Emergence:** Your colony develops geography that tells its story. New colonists learn the lore.

**Tension:** None—pure flavor, but makes the world feel lived-in.

## The Void Between

**Layer:** 2 → 3

**Fantasy:** Interstellar travel is dangerous. Ships can be lost. Sometimes they come back... changed.

**Mechanic:** Ships traveling between stars have a small chance of "incidents." Lost ships might reappear years later with strange cargo, changed crews, or warnings.

**Emergence:** Players dread certain routes. Legends form around ghost ships. Successful crossings feel earned.

**Tension:** Fast expansion vs. safe consolidation. Do you send the colony ship through the dangerous route?

## Physical Economy

**Layer:** Cross-layer

**Fantasy:** Watching your empire breathe as fleets of freighters ferry goods between worlds.

**Mechanic:** Resources are items, not numbers. They exist at specific coordinates (stockpiles, ships). To use iron from Planet A on Planet B, a ship must load it, travel, and unload it.

**Emergence:** A pirate blockade doesn't just lower income; it literally starves a planet. You can visually track a specific crate of food from farm to table across lightyears.

**Tension:** Do you build efficient specialized worlds (vulnerable to disruption) or inefficient self-sufficient ones?

## Dynasties & Lineage

**Layer:** 1

**Fantasy:** Seeing the descendants of your original crew lead your empire centuries later.

**Mechanic:** Pops have parents. Genetic traits and social reputation are passed down. Important families may emerge naturally.

**Emergence:** A "royal family" might form purely because the first captain's kids got good educations. A genetic flaw might plague a specific clan.

**Tension:** Do you assign jobs based on merit or respect the "noble" families to keep the peace?

---

## Colonial Drift

**Layer:** 3

**Fantasy:** The struggle to keep a far-flung empire united against the ravages of distance and time.

**Mechanic:** Colonies accumulate "Drift" based on distance from the capital and lack of communication. High drift leads to divergent ethics, new factions, and eventually independence movements.

**Emergence:** Your distant science colony, isolated by a warp storm, develops a technocratic culture that views your central government as obsolete.

**Tension:** Invest in expensive communication infrastructure to enforce conformity, or allow colonies to evolve (and potentially secede)?
