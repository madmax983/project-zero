## Pop Memory

**Layer:** 1

**Fantasy:** Pops remember what happened to them. A pop who survived a famine distrusts food supplies. A pop who was in the first colony ship has prestige.

**Mechanic:** Pops accumulate "memories" from events they witness. Memories influence behavior and mood. Shared memories create culture.

**Emergence:** Veterans of hard times might be more resilient—or more traumatized. Colony identity forms from shared experience.

**Tension:** Do you shield pops from hardship or let struggle forge character?

## Named Locations

**Layer:** 1

**Fantasy:** Places earn names from what happened there. "Famine Field." "The Old Mine." "Founders' Square."

**Mechanic:** Tiles where significant events occur get named. Names persist. Pops reference names. Player can see history by clicking a named place.

**Emergence:** Your colony develops geography that tells its story. New colonists learn the lore.

**Tension:** None—pure flavor, but makes the world feel lived-in.

## The Void Between

**Layer:** 2 → 3

**Fantasy:** Interstellar travel is dangerous. Ships can be lost. Sometimes they come back... changed.

**Mechanic:** Ships traveling between stars have a small chance of "incidents." Lost ships might reappear years later with strange cargo, changed crews, or warnings.

**Emergence:** Players dread certain routes. Legends form around ghost ships. Successful crossings feel earned.

**Tension:** Fast expansion vs. safe consolidation. Do you send the colony ship through the dangerous route?

## The Rumor Web

**Layer:** 1

**Fantasy:** The colony feels like a small town where everyone knows everyone's business—even if it's wrong.

**Mechanic:** Pops carry "knowledge tokens" (resource locations, job openings, events). When adjacent, they swap tokens. Some tokens are "False Rumors" generated by low mood or traits.

**Emergence:** A false rumor about a food shortage triggers a run on the granary, causing an *actual* shortage.

**Tension:** Do you suppress free speech (limit gathering) to stop panic, or trust the truth to win out?

## Heirloom Tech

**Layer:** 1

**Fantasy:** You are stragglers with advanced tech you cannot reproduce. The "Old World" machines are magic to you.

**Mechanic:** You start with a few high-efficiency buildings (Fusion Reactor, Omni-Fabricator) that degrade over time. You lack the tech to repair them initially.

**Emergence:** The crisis when the "Old Reactor" finally dies defines the mid-game transition from scavenging to industrialization.

**Tension:** Run the old machines hard for early growth, or throttle them to make them last?

## Orbital Debris

**Layer:** 2

**Fantasy:** The consequences of your industrial expansion trap you on the planet.

**Mechanic:** Every ship launch, destruction, or orbital construction generates "debris" probability in orbit. High debris risks destroying new launches.

**Emergence:** A Kessler Syndrome event locks you on the surface just as you need to evacuate or trade. You must invent "janitor ships."

**Tension:** Cheap, dirty boosters vs. expensive, clean launches. Speed vs. sustainability.

---

## Signal Latency

**Layer:** Cross-Layer

**Fantasy:** Ruling a galactic empire means dealing with the speed of light. You are an Emperor, not a god.

**Mechanic:** Orders issued to Layer 2/3 entities take time to arrive based on distance. Intel reports are similarly delayed.

**Emergence:** You send a fleet to quell a rebellion, but by the time they arrive, the rebels have already surrendered—or taken the system.

**Tension:** Do you micromanage with lag, or delegate autonomy to AI governors who might disobey?
